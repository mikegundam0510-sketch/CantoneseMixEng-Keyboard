## HKCanCor and Rime Cantonese

HKCanCor: Luke, Kang Kwong & Wong, May L. Y. (2015), The Hong Kong Cantonese Corpus. Source https://github.com/fcbond/hkcancor at 39aeadf920e0b5ca93d0ad7792c59e740e7bdd65, data/hkcancor-utf8.zip. CC BY 4.0, bundled in licenses/HKCANCOR-CC-BY-4.0.txt.

Rime Cantonese: CanCLID and contributors, https://github.com/rime/rime-cantonese at 259f0e48bba840c3a2e0d117539e96937f3d89bc, jyut6ping3.words.dict.yaml. CC BY 4.0, bundled in licenses/RIME-CANTONESE-CC-BY-4.0.txt.

Modified data: Quick-code vocabulary cantonese_phrases.tsv and statistical five-gram model language_model.b64, generated by tools/train_language_model.py. Conversation files are split before training; held-out and validation conversations do not add training phrases. The model also includes the existing LGPL-3.0 Rime Essay vocabulary and project-authored priorities. All source notices and licenses remain applicable to their respective contributions; these statistical modifications are not endorsed by the original authors. Model revisions, hashes and evaluation are recorded in MODEL_REPORT.json.

Additional modified HKCanCor data: `phrase_model.b64` contains bounded evidence derived from the corpus's original segmented-word bigrams and trigrams, using only the same training conversations. Corpus attribution and CC BY 4.0 remain applicable. `tools/train_phrase_model.py` records the input checksum, excluded conversations and transformation in `PHRASE_MODEL_REPORT.json`. Project-authored synthetic chat scenarios in `tools/data/hk_chat_scenarios.json` contribute a documented prior; these are not collected conversations or measured Hong Kong usage frequencies. Only their training split contributes to the model and `hk_chat_phrases.tsv` vocabulary. Validation and held-out scenario families are excluded. The original five-gram model remains unchanged; the new word-transition evidence is an additional scorer, not an endorsement by the corpus authors.

`candidate_ranker.b64` additionally learns signed two/three-character relations from training-only HKCanCor utterances and authored training families, contrasted with actual exact-code candidates produced by the pinned baseline decoder. HKCanCor attribution and CC BY 4.0 remain applicable to its contribution; the existing dictionary/vocabulary notices also apply to competing candidate sources. No private user messages are used. Input pairs, split manifest and baseline revision are in `tools/data/candidate_ranking/`; `tools/train_candidate_ranker.py` records the transformation and input hashes in `CANDIDATE_RANKER_REPORT.json`. Multiple competing candidates from the same positive utterance, or variants of the same authored family, count as one observation when deciding feature support. Validation and held-out text never contributes training relations.


`word_ranker.b64` is a further modified statistical model derived from the same training-only HKCanCor word segmentation and coarse part-of-speech annotations, project-authored training families, and the pinned competing-candidate pairs. HKCanCor attribution and CC BY 4.0 apply to its contribution; dictionary/vocabulary candidate notices remain applicable. `tools/train_word_ranker.py` learns signed two/three-word collocations and shares evidence through training-only majority POS and lexical anchors, compiling the result into bounded surface weights. This is not a syntactic parser and does not assert that negative alternatives are impossible. Input hashes, exclusions and transformations are in `WORD_RANKER_REPORT.json`. No validation/held-out conversation or private user text is used for fitting.
